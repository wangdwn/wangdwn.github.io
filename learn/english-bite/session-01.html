The Quiet Superpower of Walking

放学路上的十分钟，为什么往往比刷十分钟短视频更「值钱」？

~520 词 ~7 分钟 10 点词 朱砂 高考常考

After the last bell, most students reach for their phones before they reach for the bus door. A short video promises a quick laugh. Then another. Ten minutes disappear, and the mind still feels restless.

Try a different routine. Put the phone in your pocket and walk for those same ten minutes. You do not need a park or a perfect sunset. A noisy street is enough. What matters is the rhythm of your steps.

Walking looks simple, but it quietly trains attention. When you scroll, your eyes jump every few seconds. When you walk, your body settles into a steady beat. Thoughts that felt stuck often begin to move. A math problem you could not finish, a sentence you wanted to write, even a worry you tried to ignore—these ideas sometimes return with a clearer shape.

Scientists who study the brain have long noticed a link between gentle movement and creative thinking. You do not have to memorize their papers. You can test the idea yourself. Tomorrow, after school, walk one bus stop farther than usual. Leave your headphones off for that stretch. Let your eyes glance at shop signs, trees, and faces, without hunting for a “like.”

Some students say walking feels like a waste of time. They want every free minute to be “useful.” But usefulness is not only about finishing worksheets. Restoring focus is useful. Noticing the world is useful. Building a small habit you can keep is useful. A walk is a deliberate pause, not an empty gap.

If your friends persuade you to stay online, you can still protect a short walk. Tell them you will join the group chat in ten minutes. Most people accept a clear boundary more easily than a soft “maybe later.” You do not need to argue. You only need to start moving.

Of course, walking will not solve every problem. It will not replace sleep, or honest effort in class. But it can reduce the feeling that your day is only a chain of screens. Over a week, those quiet ten-minute walks add up. You may sleep a little better. You may find it easier to absorb a reading passage at night. You may even discover that boredom is not an enemy—it is a door.

Start small. Do not wait for motivation to arrive like a gift. Motivation often follows action. Lace your shoes, step outside, and give your mind a chance to breathe. The superpower is quiet. That is why so many people miss it.

One more tip for exam season: if you hesitate before opening a thick vocabulary list, walk first. After you return, open the list for just fifteen minutes. You will often find that your brain is warmer, less distracted, and more willing to work. That is not magic. That is a body helping a mind.

So tonight, when the bus is crowded and your thumb is ready to scroll, try the quieter choice. Ten minutes of walking will not make headlines. It may, however, give you back a little space to think—and that space is where real insight begins.
