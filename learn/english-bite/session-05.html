Session 05 · 气候·海洋 · B1–B2

When Color Leaves the Reef

礁石变白，不是被洗洁精洗过——那是海洋用颜色发出的警报。

~280 词 ~5 分钟 9 点词 朱砂 高考常考
本篇怎么读
  1. 先通读英文抓大意；卡壳再点蓝色/朱砂词。
  2. 释义里若出现「词根馆」链接，可跳去巩固同一词根。
  3. 读完做配对、填空、用法、限时回忆（约两分钟）。

Coral reefs look like stone gardens, but they are busy cities of life. Tiny animals build the hard structure. Inside their tissue live colorful algae that share food made from sunlight.

When ocean temperature stays too high for too long, the partnership breaks. Stressed coral pushes the algae out. Without their color partners, the reef turns ghost-white. People call this bleaching—though no cleaner is involved.

A white reef is not always dead, but it is hungry and weak. If heat returns often, whole sections can collapse. Fish lose shelter. Coastal towns lose a natural wave shield. An ecosystem that took centuries to grow can thin out in a few harsh summers.

The story sounds distant if you live far from the sea. Yet climate links classrooms to coasts. The same physics that warms a closed room—extra energy trapped in the system—plays out across the planet’s water. Measuring, modeling, and honest data are how we see the trend.

Hope is not a slogan here. Some teams try to restore damaged patches with heat-tougher coral. Others cut local pollution so reefs can breathe. None of this replaces cutting emissions, but small recoveries prove care still matters.

If you ever sketch a simple energy diagram in class—sunlight in, heat trapped, water warming—you already hold a piece of the reef story. When color fades, the ocean is sending an urgent message in plain sight. Reading that message is part of growing up in this century.

Connecting textbook energy arrows to a living coastline is one way English reading and science thinking can travel together—without waiting for a field trip to begin.

Evidence first; slogans later.
