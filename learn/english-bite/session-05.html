When Color Leaves the Reef

珊瑚白化：高温拆散共生藻——颜色走了，生态压力来了。

~335 词 ~7 分钟 高二 深度 ★★★★☆ 难度 ★★★☆☆ 朱砂 高考常考
本篇怎么读
  1. 先通读英文抓大意；卡壳再点蓝色/朱砂词。
  2. 释义里若出现「词根馆」链接，可跳去巩固同一词根。
  3. 读完做配对、填空、用法、限时回忆与推断题（约两分钟）。

Coral reefs look like underwater cities built from stone and color. The color, however, is not only rock. It comes largely from tiny algae living inside coral tissues. In a healthy partnership, the algae share food made by photosynthesis, and the coral provides shelter.

When ocean temperatures rise for too long, the partnership breaks down. Stressed corals expel their algae partners. Without the algae, the white skeleton shows through—a process reporters call bleaching. Bleaching does not always mean instant death, but it means the coral is running on empty and more vulnerable to disease.

Why should a high-school student in a coastal Chinese city care? Because reefs protect shorelines, support fisheries, and store lessons about symbiosis—the idea that two different organisms can share a fate. When heat waves become more frequent, whole neighborhoods of reef can tip from colorful to pale within weeks.

Local clubs, museums, and school science fairs can turn bleaching from a distant headline into a measurable question: what temperature histories sit above the reefs we still have, and which human choices move those numbers?

Scientists monitor sea-surface temperatures and warn of bleaching risk the way weather apps warn of storms. The language is careful: risk is not destiny. Some reefs recover if waters cool and water quality stays decent. Others collapse into rubble fields where fish lose habitat and local economies lose a living museum.

Solutions sound familiar from other climate topics: cut greenhouse gas emissions, reduce local pollution, and protect remaining healthy reefs as “seed banks” for recovery. None of these is a single homework worksheet. All of them reward the same reading skill you practice here—connecting a vivid scene to a larger system.

Next time you see a postcard of blue water and bright coral, read it as a temporary balance. Color on a reef is a contract between heat, light, and living partners. When the contract fails, the ocean does not shout. It simply turns pale—and asks whether humans noticed in time.
