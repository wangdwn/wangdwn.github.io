Session 03 · 生物 · B1–B2

Earth’s Quiet Tough Guys

地球上最「扛造」的选手，可能小到要用显微镜才看得见。

~283 词 ~5 分钟 9 点词 朱砂 高考常考
本篇怎么读
  1. 先通读英文抓大意；卡壳再点蓝色/朱砂词。
  2. 释义里若出现「词根馆」链接，可跳去巩固同一词根。
  3. 读完做配对、填空、用法、限时回忆（约两分钟）。

If you had to vote for Earth’s toughest animal, you might pick a lion. Scientists often smile and name a creature you can barely see: the tardigrade, also called a water bear.

Tardigrades are microscopic. Many live in damp moss. They look almost comic—short legs, slow walk—yet they can survive conditions that would end most species.

In labs, researchers have dried them out, frozen them, and even exposed them to the near-vacuum of space. Some revive when water returns. Their trick is not magic armor. They enter a special paused state, packing their cells so damage slows down. That makes them strangely resilient.

Why should a high-school science student care? Because extreme life rewrites our guess about where biology can work. If tiny animals can handle radiation and cold, then life on other worlds seems a little less impossible—and research on Earth’s harshest places becomes more exciting.

Tardigrades also teach a study habit in disguise. When a problem feels impossible, you do not always need more force. Sometimes you need a smarter pause: break the task, protect what matters, and restart with water—or with a clearer plan.

Of course they are not comic-book superheroes; labs report careful numbers, not legends. Still, next to most animals their recovery looks astonishing. Next time you look through a school microscope, remember: the potential champion of survival may be waiting in a drop of moss water, quietly rewriting what “strong” means.

Ask what the pause protects, and you are already thinking like a biologist. Toughness is not always loud; sometimes it is a careful restart when conditions allow life to move again.

Quiet toughness still counts as data.
